# First campaign: devices 3 + 4 alone do not make our instances findable

Run 2026-09-01 evening on a rented 96-core box (RunPod A40 host, about 0.65 $ total),
so the laptop's own timed measurements ran undisturbed; raw logs in the job folder
`work/2026-09-01_shaped-encodings/out/pod-campaign-records.tgz` on the machine.

## What ran

250 shaped `matmul_3x3x3 --target 23` XNF instances from this branch's generator
(pairing under the derived 19x1 + 4x2 quota; zeroing at 0, 6, 12, 18, 25, 37, 50 %;
streamline seeds 1-45), filtered and searched with xnfsat (`--witness=1`, its native
XNF mode). Controls first: two of Heule's challenge1 instances solve in the same
harness on the same box, so the pipeline is valid.

## The numbers

| stage | result |
|---|---|
| 5 s propagation filter | **197 of 250 instantly UNSAT** (exit 20, ~0.03 s) |
| survivors | 53: every pairing-only instance, plus 8 at zeroing <= 18 % |
| 300 s x 3 seeds on the 53 | 0 finds of 159 runs |
| 900 s x 1 seed on the 45 pairing-only | 0 finds of 45 |

## What the deaths taught (the campaign's real yield)

**The cross-term channel.** A product that owns two odd entries has their alpha,
beta, gamma forced through the shared `q` and `c` variables, which forces *other*
triple terms on that product; a zeroing draw (or an exclusivity unit) that hits a
forced term refutes the formula by propagation alone. Four doubles per instance
make almost every draw die this way at any real zeroing rate, which is why the
zeroing x pairing cross kills 197 of 250 and plausibly why the source's own
generation was discard-and-retry. Two fixes follow:

1. **Zero only the free terms**: compute the pairing's propagation closure first
   and exclude the forced terms from the zeroing pool - algebra-aware zeroing,
   cheap to add to `streamlining.cpp`.
2. **Reject at birth**: a 5 s solver call per candidate separates dead from
   searching for pennies; the generator should loop seeds until a quota of
   searching instances exists (their discipline, mechanised).

## What the negative means, honestly

Devices 3 + 4 as published are **not sufficient** to reproduce solvable shaped
instances at this budget: the claim "shaping moves xnfsat from zero to found"
stands unproven, tested and negative for this device subset. The review already
names what the solvable originals carry beyond it: the SAT 2019 row/column-zero
streamliners, the "each even term occurs zero times or twice" occurrence
constraint, ~50 % neighborhood fixing from a known solution (device 6, which needs
a scheme from `flip_graph/` first), and survival filtering over far more than 45
candidates. Those are the campaign's next devices, in that order; the quotient
device is unaffected by any of this and still owed its orbit argument.

## Device 6 calibration, later the same night: a cliff at half

Neighborhood fixing from Laderman's scheme (the public dataset's file, third matrix
arbitrated as transposed by the Brent equations), one seed, 60 s cap, kissat on the
plain 3-cut and xnfsat on the fixed XNF:

| fixed | kissat | xnfsat |
|---|---|---|
| 50 % | found, 0.34 s | nothing |
| 48 % | found, 2.4 s | nothing |
| 45 % | nothing | nothing |
| 40 % to 10 % | nothing | nothing |

Two readings. The threshold is a cliff just under half - the source's ~50 % choice
replicates as the practically minimal anchor, with the cost already 7x higher two
points below it. And the device lands on the CDCL side of the plan's predicted split:
half a known solution is food for unit propagation and worth nothing to the walk at
this cap, the mirror of symmetry freedom feeding walks and starving CDCL. The
walk-favoring shape is still owed by the devices not yet built.
