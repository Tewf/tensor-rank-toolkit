# What the descent reaches, and what step 3 costs

The four polynomial fixtures, with the three steps priced separately. Times
are cumulative on one core, regenerated by
[`reproduce/measure.py`](../../../reproduce/measure.py) and stored with their
provenance in [`results.json`](results.json). Protocol:
[`MEASURING.md`](../../../MEASURING.md).

| Map | Naive | Step 1 | Step 2 | Step 3 | Published |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| F2 5×5 | 25 | 16 | 14 | **14** · 0.0985 s | 13, `[bdez2012]` |
| F2 3×8 | 24 | 19 | 16 | **15** · 0.207 s | no solution at 14 |
| F2 4×7 | 28 | 19 | 16 | **16** · 0.397 s | no solution at 14 |
| F3 3×6 | 18 | 12 | 11 | **10** · 2.69 s | 10, `[bdez2012]` |

The first row, reproduced live rather than from `results.json`: `minimise-rank
fixtures/f2_5x5.tensor --steps 3` prints

    fixtures/f2_5x5.tensor
      naive: 25 multiplications, 9 slices, 0 s cumulative
      step 1: 16 multiplications, 9 slices, 0.000107719 s cumulative
      step 2: 14 multiplications, 11 slices, 0.00126404 s cumulative
    # step 3 pool: 961 rank-one maps
    # step 3 shortlist: 0
      step 3: 14 multiplications, 11 slices, 0.106819 s cumulative
    # algorithm: 14 products, rank bound 12, gap 2, L is 14x5, R is 14x5, P is 9x14

which agrees with the table above on every count; the seconds differ because
this run is a single fresh sample rather than the fastest of three under
[`MEASURING.md`](../../../MEASURING.md)'s protocol.

**Step 3 is priced badly.** It improved the answer in two of four cases, by one
product each time, at one to two orders of magnitude what steps 1 and 2 cost
together: on F2 5×5 it spent 0.0985 s confirming the 14 step 2 already had. The
range behind that is 36 to 189 times and it is **not quoted to two digits**,
because steps 1 and 2 are now 0.9 ms to 23.8 ms and a hand run moved one
fixture's ratio from 189 to 85 with nothing changed in the code
([`../MEASURING.md`](../../../MEASURING.md)).
